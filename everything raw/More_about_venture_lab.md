## 3. The structure: Innovate, Startup, Scale Up (plus "Our Approach")

Venture Lab organises everything along a journey. It also has a method called the **Venture Lab Compass**.

### 3.1 Our Approach and the Compass

- Venture Lab describes its approach as providing three things: **inspiration** (igniting passion for purpose-driven innovation and value creation), **self-belief** (raising aspirations and building confidence) and **empowerment** (the knowledge, resources and connections to turn ideas into reality).
- It publishes a set of **principles** describing the mindset it wants participants to adopt.
- The **Compass** maps the stages of innovation and entrepreneurship that Venture Lab's programmes cover. Students on key programmes **self-assess their competencies** against the stages they are working in and track their development.
- The earliest stages are self-reflection and problem exploration. The latest are launch and early-stage growth.

Source: https://www.durham.ac.uk/study/careers-employability-enterprise/our-services/start-new-ventures/our-approach

### 3.3 Startup

**Blueprint Startup Challenge** (flagship competition)
- Durham's longest-running business ideas competition. A 2023 page described it as being in its **21st year**.
- Run by the Venture Lab team. Elements: expert-led workshops, 1:1 advice, networking and social events, co-working space, grant funding.
- Prize fund described three ways on different pages: "over £20,000" (older), "£35,000" (2025/26 competition page), "£50,000" (2023 page and the current page). Individual finalist prizes run **£1,000 to £10,000**.
- Additional awards seen on older pages: **Illuminate PGR Pitch Award** (top prize £5,000) and **Social Impact Award** (up to £5,000), plus awards for engineers.
- Finalists get mentoring from the Durham global alumni community.
- Funded by gifts from alumni Richard King and David Gledhill, Santander Universities and ESBF.
- 2023 competition format: year-long, running over Michaelmas and Epiphany terms, weekly workshops, "Meet the Entrepreneur" sessions with alumni (including a founding partner of Spotify), Grand Final in June.
- **Current status:** the page says it **returns in 2027**.
- Entry scale: a 2025 regional news item said one finalist made the top six out of **more than 90** startup entries.

**Durham Venture School** (graduates)
- A pre-accelerator for graduates, described as the first of its kind in the UK higher education sector at launch.
- Package: **£1,000 per month stipend for the first three months**, pitch for **£10,000** initial funding, coaching from experienced entrepreneurs, alumni mentoring, and visits to high-growth businesses (US, London and North East). Past cohorts travelled to London, New York and Silicon Valley.
- **You do not need a business idea.** The Venture Lab team helps generate one, using proven tools to explore significant problems.
- Length: three or six months depending on team progress.
- Eligibility: Durham graduates, including those graduating in the current year. The 2026 round is open to UK and international graduates.
- 2026 timing conflict: one page says the programme **starts in September 2026**, another says **August 2026**.
- Earlier cohort pattern: the main programme included a two-day in-person bootcamp, then in-person weeks, then optional continuing in the Lab or remotely.
- Competitive: selected applicants do the initial three-month phase, then pitch for the £10,000 and a further three months.

**Other Startup services**
- **Startup coaching** sessions (1:1).
- **Hazan Venture Lab** co-working and meeting rooms.
- **Venture Lab Summer Accelerator (VLSA)**: helps aspiring entrepreneurs progress ideas. A 2024 cohort photographed in matching hoodies. Detailed terms not retrieved.
- **Innovator Founder Visa** guidance: route for international graduates to launch a UK company after graduating. Details not retrieved.
- **Durham City Incubator**: partnership with Durham County Council and New College Durham (mentioned in the 2022 KEF page).

### 3.4 Scale Up

Powered by Durham's global alumni community. Connects early-stage founders to entrepreneurs, investors and industry leaders to help them set ambitious goals, prepare for investment and growth, and unlock markets.

- **Durham Venture Mentors:** mentoring from alumni and friends.
- **Durham University Alumni Entrepreneurs Network:** described as one of Durham's most active alumni groups. Runs regular events for aspiring, current and former entrepreneurs, investors and sector leaders.
- **Venture Lab Angel Network** (also the **Durham Alumni Angel Syndicate**): pitch to alumni angel investors for early-stage investment. Durham alumni investors found Low Carbon Materials through the "Durham University Angels" network (per Ada Ventures).
- **Venture Lab London Pop Ups:** a community space for Durham alumni founders based in London.
- **Durham Entrepreneur's Pledge:** founders pledge to support the next generation or give back later.

Source: https://dur.ac.uk/study/careers-employability-enterprise/our-services/start-new-ventures/scale-up/

## 4. Teaching and academic side

Venture Lab itself runs **workshops, coaching, programmes and competitions**, not degree modules. I found these teaching-related items:

- **Workshops** are a core part of Blueprint (weekly in the 2023 format) and Game Changer. Topics named: business fundamentals, design thinking, rapid prototyping, pitching.
- **Mini MBA Innovation Challenge** at Durham University Business School: the Low Carbon Materials founders took part in this while doing PhDs, and credit it as the origin of their idea.
- **MSc Management (Entrepreneurship)**, Durham University Business School: a 12-month taught masters. Core modules include strategy, organisational behaviour, introduction to entrepreneurship, new venture creation and thinking entrepreneurially. It accepts graduates from any discipline.
- Durham Business School is also a route used by Tred's founders for early grants and coaching.

I did **not** find a published list of credit-bearing enterprise modules offered through Venture Lab itself. If you need that, ask Venture Lab or the Business School directly.

## 6. Enactus Durham (student-led social enterprise)

- Supported by Venture Lab. Rachel Bickerdike is the University Adviser.
- A Durham Student Organisation with **210 members** in 2023 and several projects tackling around 11 UN SDGs.
- **UK & Ireland champions in 2023 and 2024.** Represented the UK at the Enactus World Cup in **Utrecht (October 2023)** and **Astana, Kazakhstan (October 2024)**, where they placed in the top 16.
- Projects named in sources: **Taka Taka Zero** (waste to energy in an informal settlement in Nairobi), **The Ugly Fruit Group** (reducing food waste and insecurity in County Durham), **Glowcycle** (employment for previously incarcerated women in Durham), **Trashion** (fashion made from waste), **The Durham Wardrobe Collective** (pre-loved professional clothing plus careers advice).
- Taka Taka Zero attracted a **£30,000 donation** from the Ford Family Foundation after the 2023 national win.
- Venture Lab, the Student Enrichment Team and Durham University Business School helped fund the World Cup trip.
- The goal stated for 2026 was to reach the World Cup in Brazil.

## 7. What Venture Lab looks for in a project

**Important:** I found no published scoring sheet for Blueprint, Venture School or Hunt-Raymond. The list below is drawn from Durham's own stated priorities, ordered by how often they recur. The last two items are my inference from winners.

1. **Positive impact on the world.** A Durham profile on the national enterprise-educators network says an intention to have positive impact is one of the key criteria Blueprint judges assess entrants against. The brochure says Venture Lab startup focus is "sustainable innovation with the potential to scale."
2. **Commercial viability and scalability.** The same profile says considering how commercially viable and scalable ventures can create impact is a core thread through all Durham activity.
3. **Significant problems.** Venture School is framed around exploring major global and industry challenges and turning them into ventures. Game Changer is anchored to the UN SDGs. Hunt-Raymond lists priority science and technology themes.
4. **Mindset and growth.** Venture Lab's Compass and principles stress self-belief, and participants track their own competency development.
5. **Any stage is welcome (for Blueprint).** A 2023 page said Blueprint was open to students with any startup idea at any stage of development. Venture School does not require an idea at all.
6. **Interdisciplinary teams** are explicitly encouraged in Hunt-Raymond.
7. **Novelty and potential impact** are named selection criteria for the PhD feasibility scholarship.
8. **(My inference) A distinctive technology or product angle.** The named winners (biochar fertiliser, carbon-negative aggregates, AI video tools, a skin-tone matching device) tend to have something concrete and differentiated.
9. **(My inference) Evidence of progress.** Many success stories mention pilots, grants, accelerator places or early customers, which suggests momentum is valued, though Durham does not state it as a rule.

**Practical advice for an application (my suggestion, not Durham's):** state the problem and who suffers from it; show how your solution is different; give a believable route to scale; link to an SDG or measurable impact; and show you have already tested something, however small.
