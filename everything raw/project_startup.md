## How to read this document

**About "find the business plan online".** I searched for each company's published plan or investor pitch. **No full, formal business plan for any of these companies is publicly available**, which is normal: private companies rarely publish them. Instead, each profile uses the closest public equivalents: investor campaign pages, crowdfunding pitches, founder interviews, press coverage, grant announcements and Durham's own case studies.

Each profile follows a business-plan layout (problem, solution, market, model, traction, funding, team, risks, status). Sections are marked:

- **Reported:** taken from a source, with a link.
- **Not found:** I looked and could not find it. I have not guessed.
- **My assessment:** my own reading, clearly labelled. Treat as opinion.

Numbers come from different dates. Where sources conflict, both are shown.

## PROJECT 1: Alora (formerly Agrisea)

**One-line summary:** Gene-edited rice (and later other crops) that tolerates seawater so it can be grown on ocean-surface farms or on salt-damaged land.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
- Rice farming produces large quantities of methane, and lowland rice areas are suffering salt intrusion from sea-level rise. The founders cite the Mekong Delta as an example.
- A significant share of agricultural land worldwide suffers some salinity harm. The CEO described it as getting close to a third of farmland.
- Rice agriculture is described as responsible for around 1.5 percent of global greenhouse gas emissions in one regional article.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- Alora uses CRISPR-based editing to "switch on" a set of eight genes that are normally active only in naturally salt-tolerant plants such as seagrasses, giving rice high salt tolerance. A utility patent has been filed on the eight-gene approach.
- The company says its crops are not GMOs because no foreign DNA is introduced (per Toyota Ventures' investment post).
- The original concept paired the plants with a floating ocean farm design. Plants draw nutrients from seawater, similar to hydroponics.
- An Ontario trial (University of Guelph) grew plants in water at 8 g of salt per litre. Seawater is roughly 28 to 32 g per litre. The CEO said he aimed to push tolerance toward 28 g.
- An earlier regional article claimed tolerance of up to 50 percent of ocean salinity.

### 3. Market (Reported / My assessment)
- Targets: coastal lowland rice regions, salt-affected soils, and potentially new production area on water. The Food Planet Prize entry says it develops multiple varieties for climates from North America to Europe to Southern Africa to Southeast Asia and aims to expand into other grains, legumes, fruits and vegetables.
- *My assessment:* the practical buyers are likely seed companies, governments and large growers rather than consumers, so licensing and partnership routes matter. Public sources do not state the commercial model.

### 4. Business model (Not found)
No pricing or licensing model is published. Early statements say the company hoped to install a first farm and complete a Series A.

### 5. Traction and milestones (Reported)
- Founded 2019 by two Durham biology students.
- Graduated from **IndieBio** (SOSV), San Francisco.
- Seed round announced April 2022: **$1.4m** (Toyota Ventures, Mistletoe and others).
- A later article reports **$3.72m** raised in total seed funding from investors including Toyota Ventures, Sustainable Oceans Alliance and Mistletoe, aiming for a Series A.
- Durham's October 2024 article cites over **$3.5m** raised.
- Norwich Research Park coverage said the company was preparing a Series A of **$15 to 30m USD** (article undated, around 2023).
- Food Planet Prize runner-up 2021; Future Food Asia finalist 2020. Featured in Forbes and Wired. Luke Young was a One Young World scholar in 2024.
- Bahamas pilot referenced in the prize entry as a planned project.

### 6. Funding (Reported)
Investors named: Toyota Ventures, Mistletoe, Neglected Climate Opportunities, SOSV and IndieBio, Next Humanity Ventures, Velocity, and angels (including Jude Gomila and Tamara Minick-Scokalo).

### 7. Team (Reported)
Luke Young (CEO and co-founder; plant genetics and hormone regulation) and Rory Hornby (CSO / head of R&D; developmental biology).

### 8. Competitors (Reported)
Crunchbase lists "Seawater Solutions" as a possible alternative.

### 9. Risks (My assessment)
Regulation of gene-edited crops varies by country; moving from lab trials to open-water farms is unproven at commercial scale; the farm concept and the gene-edit technology are two different bets.

### 10. Status
Described as a Canada-based company (Kitchener, Ontario) in recent coverage, earlier in Norwich Research Park.

**Public business plan:** Not found. Closest items: the Toyota Ventures investment note and AgFunderNews profile.

## PROJECT 2: Low Carbon Materials (LCM), formerly Sphera

**One-line summary:** Carbon-negative aggregates (OSTO for concrete, ACLA for asphalt) that lower the carbon footprint of construction without changing existing processes.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
Construction materials, especially concrete and asphalt, carry heavy embodied carbon. The UK has a legally binding net zero target for 2050, and asphalt for roads is a major emissions source.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- **OSTO**: LCM's first product, a carbon-negative lightweight aggregate that replaces conventional lightweight aggregates in concrete.
- **ACLA**: carbon-negative aggregate for asphalt, launched in **March 2024**. Each tonne of ACLA is reported to take 750 kg of CO2 from the atmosphere as it is made. Replacing about 5 to 7 percent of an asphalt mix with ACLA can produce net zero asphalt.
- A biochar-mineral aggregate. It featured in a net zero concrete mix at the RHS Chelsea Flower Show (with Getjar and Tarmac).
- LCM stresses that its materials slot into existing mixing and laying processes.

### 3. Market (Reported)
Asphalt manufacturers, contractors, local councils and national road operators. Named customers and partners include Durham County Council, MGL Group (Tynedale Roadstone and Rainton Construction), Tarmac, Skanska, Holcim UK, Marlborough Highways (Barnet Council scheme), Murphy (a car park scheme) and Enfield Council.

### 4. Business model (Reported / My assessment)
- Designs and manufactures aggregates (a product-sales model), supplied to asphalt and concrete producers.
- *My assessment:* revenue grows with adoption by asphalt producers and public road authorities; carbon-credit revenue is not mentioned in the sources I found.

### 5. Traction (Reported)
- First use of ACLA in a UK road: **Elvet Hill, Durham** (March/April 2024), with Durham County Council.
- Motorway trial on National Highways' network (July 2024). National Highways' competition awarded up to £80,000 to each of four firms.
- Prince William visited LCM's Durham facilities (2024).
- Earthshot Prize finalist 2022. BE100 SCALE winner at Blue Earth Summit 2025, securing a share of a £600,000-plus fund. CEO won Innovate UK's Women in Innovation Award. Natasha Boulding and Scott Bush are Forbes 30 Under 30.
- Blueprint Startup Challenge winner 2019.

### 6. Funding (Reported)
- Ada Ventures invested (found through Durham University Angels). Its September 2024 post notes the founders are a chemist, a physicist and a mathematician.
- Northern Accelerator reports **£3m growth acceleration funding**.
- BE100 prize share (2025).

### 7. Team (Reported)
Dr Natasha Boulding (CEO), Dr Phil Buckley, Scott Bush. The three met doing PhDs at Durham and developed the idea during Durham Business School's Mini MBA Innovation Challenge. First student-led Durham spin-out, based in Seaham, County Durham.

### 8. Competitors (Reported)
CB Insights lists Adaptavate (carbon-negative building materials, Bristol) as a comparable company.

### 9. Risks (My assessment)
Dependence on public-sector procurement standards for new materials; need to prove long-term performance; scaling manufacturing.

**Public business plan:** Not found. Closest items: Ada Ventures' "Why we invested" post and LCM's news page.

## PROJECT 3: IMPOSSIBREW

**One-line summary:** A "functional" alcohol-free beer brand using a plant-based "Social Blend" designed to deliver relaxation without alcohol.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
Founder Mark Wong was told to stop drinking after being diagnosed with alcohol-induced liver damage (Beer Today, 2022). He found existing non-alcoholic beers replicated taste but not the unwinding effect.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- Craft-style non-alcoholic beers (lager, pale ale, hazy pale ale) with a patent-pending **Social Blend**, developed with Durham University biosciences professor **Paul Chazot**. It is described as combining nootropics and adaptogens with premium brewing.
- Early production was in Allendale, Northumberland.

### 3. Market (Reported)
- Positions itself in the global non-alcoholic drinks market (the company describes it as worth over $1 trillion and says non-alcoholic drinks are projected to overtake alcoholic drinks by 2026, citing Statista). Treat those as company claims.
- Sells direct-to-consumer and through retail; 2026 coverage reports a **listing with Ocado**.

### 4. Business model (Reported)
Direct-to-consumer subscriptions and orders, plus grocery retail and on-trade (taprooms). Funded partly through crowdfunding.

### 5. Traction (Reported)
- Founded 2020 in a Durham University bedroom.
- Dragons' Den, early 2022. **Conflict in sources:** Beer Today says Wong asked for £50,000 for 10 percent; the company's later release says the investment passed on was £45,000. All five Dragons declined. He then raised almost double the amount from angel investors.
- Gold at London Beer Competition 2022; World Beer Awards 2023 (UK's best non-alcoholic lager and pale ale); Great Taste awards. Startups 100 (2024) shortlisted for Exceptional Founder.
- Crowdcube, January 2025: target £500,000 reached in 35 minutes of priority access, over £1.2m in 24 hours, final **£1.4m from over 1,000 investors** at a reported **£12m pre-money valuation**. Reported sales of over 1 million cans in 2024; January 2025 monthly sales value expected around £700,000.
- 2026 reporting: turnover grew from £37,000 in year one to **£3.57m** with a team of four; more than 100,000 direct customers; 63 percent returning customers.
- A 2026 Crowdcube campaign raised **£725,631 from 784 investors** (British Guild of Beer Writers release). Dealroom described the target as about $650,000. Total raised across two earlier rounds is given as **£2.33m**.
- Durham calls IMPOSSIBREW the first Venture Lab startup to raise over £1m and reach £1m annual turnover.

### 6. Funding (Reported)
Angel investors (including a FTSE 250 CFO, per 2022 article); Crowdcube rounds as above.

### 7. Team (Reported)
Mark Wong, founder; a lean team of about four, described by the company as "AI-native."

### 8. Competitors (Reported)
Dealroom names Athletic Brewing and large-brand alcohol-free offshoots as competition for shelf space.

### 9. Risks (Reported / My assessment)
Dealroom lists a crowded alcohol-free beer market. *My assessment:* health claims around nootropics and adaptogens may attract regulatory scrutiny; check current advertising rules before any marketing claim.

**Public business plan:** Not found. Closest items: the Crowdcube campaign pages and press releases summarised in the sources below.

## PROJECT 4: Charter (Charter Space)

**One-line summary:** Software for organising spacecraft design, manufacturing and testing, now extended into a licensed space-insurance brokerage.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
Spacecraft programmes often manage logistics and engineering processes with spreadsheets, whiteboards and paper. Insuring a satellite is costly and slow because underwriters struggle to evaluate technical data.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- **Ubik**: collaborative platform covering mission planning, requirements, test procedures, system diagrams and programme records.
- A **nationally licensed insurance brokerage** launched in **May 2026**. Charter uses the engineering data to support underwriting. It is a broker, not an insurer carrying the risk.
- Planned insurance products include coverage for novel missions such as space-based nuclear power, lunar missions and in-space servicing.

### 3. Market (Reported)
Space and defence industrial base companies. Charter said it was servicing **over 50 companies** after launching the brokerage.

### 4. Business model (Reported / My assessment)
Software subscriptions plus brokerage placement. *My assessment:* brokerage fees on placed policies are likely the larger revenue driver as the business shifts, but sources do not break out revenue.

### 5. Traction (Reported)
- Co-founded late 2021 by Yuk Chi Chan and Yukun Yin. Chan is a former Singapore Army Engineers logistics officer who studied space law and worked at a space startup (Anchor Orbital) that failed, which inspired Charter.
- First British company accepted onto the **Techstars Space/Aerospace & Defense accelerator** (fall 2022 cohort). Partnered with NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. TechCrunch Startup Battlefield finalist (prior year).
- Offices: El Segundo, California; earlier plans for Los Angeles.

### 6. Funding (Reported)
- Early: $1.2m (Techstars, 7percent Ventures and others, per SpaceNews, 2022).
- CB Insights: total $5.28m at one point, investors include Hustle Fund, Gaingels, QED Investors, Underline Ventures and Techstars Ventures. A later round of about $3.96m recorded in August 2025.
- **$5m seed announced 30 September 2026**, bringing total reported funding to **$8m**.

### 7. Team (Reported)
Yuk Chi Chan (CEO), Yukun Yin (co-founder, software engineer, formerly at Credit Suisse).

### 8. Risks (My assessment)
Insurance is a regulated, relationship-driven business; a small broker depends on insurer capacity; the space insurance market is volatile.

**Public business plan:** Not found. Closest items: TechCrunch seed funding report and SpaceNews profile.

## PROJECT 5: Ground Up

**One-line summary:** Industrial biochar production hubs in India converting crop residue into carbon-removal credits and soil-improving fertiliser.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
India produces around 500 to 550 million tonnes of crop residues a year, most of which is burned or left to rot, releasing greenhouse gases and harming air quality.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- Strategically located industrial biochar hubs using pyrolysis.
- Proprietary biochar-based fertilisers that regenerate soil health and boost crop performance.
- Stated aim: make biochar accessible to every Indian farmer by **2040**.
- Operating flagship pilot: **Project Nandani**, processing sugarcane residue. Carbon Herald describes it as India's largest biochar facility.

### 3. Market (Reported)
Indian farmers (fertiliser customers), carbon-credit buyers (durable carbon removal), and industrial partners providing residue supply.

### 4. Business model (Reported)
Two revenue streams implied: sale of biochar-based fertilisers, and sale of carbon removal credits. A **pre-purchase agreement worth over $17,000** was signed with the "remove" carbon-removal accelerator, which acts as a credit buyer. A "farmer-first" approach returns biochar to local soils.

### 5. Traction (Reported)
- Founded after the founders' 2022 graduation; they joined the second **Durham Venture School** cohort, receiving workshops, 1:1 coaching, **£10,000** pre-seed funding and a US trip to meet Durham alumni entrepreneurs.
- The founders tried carbon-credit brokering and a carbon-credit data platform before settling on biochar.
- **Blueprint Startup Challenge winners 2024.**
- Durham reports a pilot with farmers in India and a significant pre-seed round.
- 2025 podcast: the founders discussed raising a **$5m seed round**. A separate paywalled report of a "$3m seed" for an Indian carbon-removal developer exists, but I could not confirm it refers to Ground Up. Treat Ground Up's seed size as unconfirmed.
- Selected for the India cohort of the "remove" accelerator and progressed to its Global Leap stage.

### 6. Team (Reported)
Rhea Dabriwala (PPE, Collingwood College, 2022) and Naveen Shivalingam (Mathematics and Statistics, Collingwood College, 2022), plus a third co-founder who joined later.

### 7. Risks (Reported / My assessment)
Carbon removal depends on credible monitoring, reporting and verification. Verra and the Advanced Carbons Council have discussed the need for credible systems and farmer inclusion in India. *My assessment:* revenue relies on carbon-market prices and buyer confidence.

**Public business plan:** Not found. Closest items: Durham case study, Carbon Herald, and a 2025 Scaling Green Tech podcast.

## PROJECT 6: Feasibly (and Local Energy Systems)

**One-line summary:** An AI geospatial platform that screens land for renewable-energy suitability in seconds.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
Feasibly says over **63 percent** of new UK renewable projects fail in planning, wasting about **£1.2bn a year** (company claim). Site feasibility work relies on slow manual workflows.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- No-code, AI-native platform that analyses grid capacity, planning constraints, land and environmental data to find grid-ready sites, using natural-language insights instead of spreadsheets and consultant reports.
- Claimed result in one deprived council area: planning costs cut by over 90 percent.

### 3. Market (Reported)
Renewable developers, local authorities and combined authorities.

### 4. Traction (Reported)
- Founded straight out of Durham. Leo Thomson built it while completing an MSc in Geospatial Data Science; Beth Holloway supported over 100 renewable projects and won an Innovate UK personal award, and sits on the North East & Yorkshire Net Zero Hub steering group.
- Two Innovate UK grants (from over 2,100 applicants).
- **£112,831** from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority to develop its geospatial AI platform.
- Graduate of **Praeseed** and **Geovation Accelerator**. Listed in the TechRound 100 (rank 44).
- One project shortlisted by the North East Mayor for government funding.

### 5. Competitors (Reported)
A similar site-selection company that appeared in my research: **REplace** (Haifa, Israel), which analyses 40-plus parameters and raised about $2.6m. Not confirmed as a direct competitor.

### 6. Note on "Local Energy Systems"
Durham pages show images captioned "Beth and Leo of Local Energy Systems," and a Durham news item lists Local Energy Systems among startups. I could not confirm whether this is an earlier name or a separate entity.

**Public business plan:** Not found. Closest items: TechRound 100 profile and Business Durham funding news.

## PROJECT 7: PolicyEngine

**One-line summary:** A free, open-source platform that models how tax and benefit policy affects households, budgets and inequality.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
Before PolicyEngine, the UK had no open-source tax-benefit microsimulation model, limiting who could examine policy proposals.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
- Web app and open datasets using microsimulation built on real household data, with US and UK versions. Users define reforms and see the effect on any household or the whole population (budget, poverty rate, net income, work incentives). Reforms are shareable by link.
- Built on the OpenFisca framework (developed by the French government's team).

### 3. Market and users (Reported)
Researchers, policymakers, think tanks across the political spectrum, and the public. Used by NIESR to measure the macro impact of cost-of-living payments.

### 4. Business model (Reported)
**Nonprofit.** Funded by grants such as the Nuffield Foundation (which has funded work on budget impacts across the UK's 650 constituencies and AI-assisted analysis). Founders initially self-funded.

### 5. Traction (Reported)
- Founded by Max Ghenis (US, former Google data scientist) and **Nikhil Woodruff**, who took a year out from his third year of Computer Science at Durham.
- Presented to parliamentarians (including Natalie Bennett, Ronnie Cowan MP and Christine Jardine MP) and at All-Party Parliamentary Groups.
- Nikhil became an Innovation Fellow with the Prime Minister's data science team at 10 Downing Street.
- Expansion plans reported in 2023: Canada, with talks on New Zealand, Israel and Nigeria.

### 6. Risks (My assessment)
Dependence on grants; model accuracy debates are inevitable; data licensing.

**Public business plan:** Not applicable (nonprofit). Closest items: the founders' "Make everyone a policy maker" essay and Nuffield grant summary.

## PROJECT 8: Hey! Food is Ready

**One-line summary:** A hyperlocal online marketplace connecting certified home cooks and chefs from many cultures with companies buying event catering.

### 1. Problem (Reported)
The founder argues mainstream catering does not reflect a multicultural society. Many refugees, immigrants and stay-at-home cooks have skills but few routes to earn income.

### 2. Solution and product (Reported)
A marketplace where certified cooks sell dishes (catering, meal delivery, cooking classes) with marketing provided. Customers get authentic dishes and often meet the chef.

### 3. Market (Reported)
Company pitch quotes **$276bn** for global catering services and food and **£8bn** for UK event catering and food service. Clients named: Google, Vogue, Condé Nast.

### 4. Business model (Reported)
**Commission on marketplace orders.**

### 5. Traction (Reported)
- Developed while the founder, Yuru Guo, completed her Master's in entrepreneurship; Newcastle Impact Incubator member from 2020; co-founders Frankie and Kiki.
- Blueprint Startup Challenge winner 2022 (£10,000).

# Part 3: Patterns across the projects (My assessment)

1. **Climate and sustainability dominate:** Alora, LCM, Ground Up, Feasibly, Tred, Taka Taka Zero.
2. **Founders often have deep technical backgrounds** (PhDs, genetics, geospatial science) and then add a business partner or cohort programme.
3. **Early cash is small, then accelerators and angels matter.** Typical sequence: Venture Lab coaching and a £1,000-£10,000 prize, then an accelerator (IndieBio, Techstars, Praeseed, Geovation), then seed funding.
4. **Several founders pivoted.** Ground Up tried carbon brokering and a carbon-data platform; Charter moved from engineering software into insurance brokerage; Tred shifted toward business accounts.
5. **Dragons' Den is a recurring exposure route** (IMPOSSIBREW, Choppity), though both founders turned declined or collapsed deals into later success or exit.
6. **Alumni networks are used as an investor pipeline** (Ada Ventures found LCM through Durham University Angels).
